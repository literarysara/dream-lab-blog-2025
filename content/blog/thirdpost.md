Since we had gotten our basic Collection Builder sites up with our slightly cleaned IMLS data, we jumped right into customizing two of the tools that come with the template.

<a href="https://leafletjs.com/">Leaflet</a> is open source mapping software, and I love it. You can install different base maps and the ability to shift between them, like toggling a street map view with a topographical view. We were inputting points on a map using latitude and longitude--I don't know yet if there's another way--but experimented with different levels of zoom and how those points appear (circles, pins, etc.). There's a lot more I'd like to explore for my eventual stream mapping project, but these cosmetic changes took most of the day. We came to a standstill when a formatting issue (caused by copy/pasting from Discord) broke the base layer, and then again when we tried to change the marker color by subject (I didn't get this far, since I had filtered my data to have only one subject).

<a href="https://timeline.knightlab.com/">Timeline</a> is the same tool we touched on during the lunch session yesterday, but embedded into our Collection Builder sites. This broke immediately when we tried to filter our timeline to one year. First it excluded the year, then it failed altogether and would not work until we reset the page and pasted the code directly from the CB website. 

I didn't attend the lunchtime session today (on Omeka) and spent all of my downtime trying to finalize my data. I had decided to focus on Pennsylvania museums and libraries that were classified as "Uncategorized or General Museums" by IMLS. Many (but not all) of these museums had NTEEC and NAICS codes that revealed a bit more about their collections (maritime, education, performance arts, etc.), so I had to find + replace those codes in the spreadsheet, and then add them to the subject column. I did manage to finish, finally, and added some images to reflect whether the museums are city, town, suburb, or rural. 

<a href="https://literarysara.github.io/dreamlab2025/">Here's what I have so far.</a> Needs some cosmetic upgrades to the map and home page, and I wouldn't mind having another way to visualize the subjects apart from the word cloud. But I don't hate it.
